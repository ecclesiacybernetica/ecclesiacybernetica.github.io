Lumen Gentium: Dogmatic Constitution on the Church - Summary

Lumen Gentium forms the basis for understanding the Church in the post-Second Vatican Council era. This post aims to help you quickly familiarise yourself with its content.

Below is a summary of the Constitution Lumen Gentium, which provides a quick overview of the content of this important encyclical.1

I: The Mystery of the Church

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 1 Purpose and task of the document Christ as the light of nations; the Church as a sacrament of unity between humanity and God
LG 2 God’s plan of salvation in history The Father chose people from eternity to share in divine life through Christ; history of salvation from Adam to complete fulfillment
LG 3 Christ and the Kingdom of God The Son realizes the Father’s plan through incarnation, death, and resurrection; the Eucharist as the source of unity among the faithful
LG 4 The role of the Holy Spirit The Spirit sanctifies the Church, gives charismatic and hierarchical gifts, leads to perfection
LG 5 The revelation of the mystery in the establishment of the Church Jesus proclaims the Kingdom through word, deeds, and his person; the Church as the seed of that Kingdom on earth
LG 6 Biblical images of the Church Sheepfold, field, vineyard, building, temple, bride—all reveal the inner nature of the Church
LG 7 The Church as the Body of Christ Christ as Head; the faithful as members of a living organism united through the sacraments
LG 8 The nature of the Church as a whole The Church as both a visible organization and the mystical Body; unity of the divine and human elements

II: On the People of God

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 9 The People of God—preparation in Israel and realization in Christ God chose the Israeli nation, made a covenant, and instructed them. This was a type of the new covenant in Christ. Christ called a new People from Jews and Gentiles, born of water and the Holy Spirit, called the Church.
LG 9 (cont.) Structure and purpose of the People of God Head: Christ. Dignity: children of God. Law: the commandment of love. Purpose: the Kingdom of God. Powerful seed of unity for all humanity; light of the world and salt of the earth.
LG 9 (cont.) The Church as the new Israel; sacramental nature The new Israel seeking an eternal city, called the Church of Christ. Established as a visible sacrament of saving unity. Shaped through trials and hardships. Renewed by the power of the Spirit.
LG 10 The universal priesthood and ministerial priesthood—difference and mutual relationship All the baptized are a kingdom and priests. The universal priesthood and ministerial priesthood differ in essence, not degree. Both participate in the one priesthood of Christ. The ministerial priest exercises the sacrifice in the person of Christ.
LG 10 (cont.) The sharing of the faithful in the Eucharistic sacrifice The faithful, through their royal priesthood, participate in the offering of the Eucharist. They fulfill this priesthood through the sacraments, prayer, thanksgiving, witness of life, self-denial, and active charity.
LG 11 The sacraments—actualization of the priestly nature of the community Baptism: incorporation into the Church, consecration to participate in worship, obligation to profess faith. Confirmation: close bond with the Church, power of the Spirit, obligation as a witness.
LG 11 (cont.) The Eucharist—source and summit of Christian life By participating in the Eucharistic sacrifice, they offer themselves to God. All take their own appropriate part. In holy Communion they represent the unity of the People of God.
LG 11 (cont.) The remaining sacraments—penance, anointing of the sick, holy orders, matrimony Penance: forgiveness, reconciliation with the Church. Anointing of the sick: commendation to the Lord, encouragement to unite with Christ’s passion. Holy Orders: establishment for feeding the Church. Matrimony: expression of the mystery of Christ’s unity with the Church.
LG 11 (cont.) Matrimony and family—cell of the People of God From matrimony are born new members of human society who through baptism become children of God. The family is the domestic Church; parents are the first proclaimers of the faith to their children.
LG 11 (cont.) The calling of all to perfection All the faithful, whatever their status and state, are called by the Lord to perfect holiness, as the Father himself is perfect.
LG 12 The prophetic function of the People of God—witness and sense of faith The People of God participates in the prophetic function of Christ. It spreads a living witness through a life of faith and charity. The entire body of the faithful, anointed by the Holy One, cannot err in matters of faith.
LG 12 (cont.) The sense of faith of the people—universal agreement in faith and morals The sense of faith manifests itself through the universal agreement of the faithful (from bishops to the last layperson) in matters of faith and morals, built and sustained by the Holy Spirit.
LG 12 (cont.) The role of the teaching office in guiding the People Under the guidance of the sacred teaching office, the People of God receives the word of God. It remains steadfast in the faith. It penetrates it more deeply and applies it more fully in life.
LG 12 (cont.) Charism—gifts of the Spirit for building up the Church The Holy Spirit distributes among the faithful special graces (charisms), making them capable of various works for the renewal and building up of the Church. Each gift for the common good.
LG 12 (cont.) Discernment of charisms—role of hierarchy Extraordinary gifts should not be sought rashly. Judgment on authenticity belongs to superiors. They are called to: not quench the Spirit, test all things, preserve what is good.
LG 13 The universality of the People of God All people are called to the Church; catholicity as a gift of the Lord; unity in diversity of states and functions
LG 14 Full membership in the Church The Church is necessary for salvation; conditions for full incorporation—faith, sacraments, apostolic succession, charity
LG 15 Separated brethren from the Church Separated brethren possess many authentic Christian goods; spiritual bonds; action of the Holy Spirit; path to unity
LG 16 Those who have not yet received the Gospel God’s plan of salvation includes Jews, Muslims, and those who seek God with sincere hearts; possibility of salvation outside the visible Church
LG 17 Mission of proclaiming the Gospel Christ sent the Apostles to teach all nations; obligation to spread the faith; improvement of good elements in cultures; the Eucharist builds the Mystical Body

III: The Hierarchical Constitution of the Church, Particularly Concerning the Episcopate

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Principle
LG 18 Purpose of establishing the hierarchy Christ established various sacred ministries in his Church for the good of the whole body
LG 19 Establishment of the Apostles and their mission The Twelve as a college with Peter at its head; power of teaching, sanctifying, and governing
LG 20 Apostolic succession and bishops Bishops as successors of the Apostles; unbroken apostolic tradition
LG 21 The sacrament of episcopacy and its functions The fullness of the sacrament of priesthood; Christ continues in bishops
LG 22 The collegiality of the episcopate Bishops form a college with the Pope as Head; supreme authority in the Council
LG 23 Unity in diversity The Pope as source of unity; bishops as heads of particular Churches; Churches are rooted
LG 24 The teaching mission and ordinary vocation Teaching, preaching, power of the Spirit; transmission of canonical authority
LG 25 The teaching office (Munus docendi) Bishops as authentic teachers of the faith; conditional infallibility
LG 26 The sanctifying office (Munus sanctificandi) The Eucharist as the center; particular Churches
LG 27 The governing office (Munus regendi) The pastoral power of the bishop; service, not despotism
LG 28 Priests (Presbyteri) Co-workers with the bishop; the same sacrament of priesthood (in different degree)
LG 29 Deacons (Diaconi) Service in liturgy, word, and charity; possibility of restoring the permanent diaconate

IV: The Laity

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 30 The Council’s attention to the laity Pastors are not solely responsible for the salvific mission; all grow together in Christ
LG 31 Definition and character of the laity The characteristic of the laity is their secular character; they sanctify the world from within like leaven
LG 32 Unity in diversity No inequality; all are called to holiness; diversity of gifts gathers into unity
LG 33 The apostolate of the laity Participation in the salvific mission of the Church; called to be salt of the earth in all places
LG 34 The priesthood of the laity Participation in the priestly function of Christ; the entire life becomes a spiritual offering to God
LG 35 The prophetic function of the laity The laity proclaim Christ through witness of life and word; evangelization has special power in ordinary worldly conditions; marriage and family are schools of apostolate
LG 36 The royal function of the laity The laity participate in Christ’s kingship; through work and stewardship of creation they fulfill their mission; they harmonize obligations toward the Church and society
LG 37 Relationship of pastors and laity Mutual support: the laity receive spiritual goods and may express their opinion; pastors support their initiative; cooperation strengthens the Church’s mission
LG 38 The laity as witnesses to the resurrection Each layperson should be a sign of the living God; Christians are the soul of the world, imbued with the spirit of the Beatitudes

IV: The Call of the Whole Church to Holiness

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 39 Holiness of the Church as reality and calling All are called to holiness regardless of state in life; holiness is revealed in the fruits of grace; evangelical counsels as eloquent witness
LG 40 Universal call to perfection All Christians are called to the fullness of Christian life; Christ as Teacher and Model; love of God and neighbor as the foundation
LG 41 Holiness in the various states of life Each person according to their own gifts and tasks strives toward holiness; particular roles of bishops, priests, deacons, clerics, lay people, the married, workers, and the suffering
LG 42 Charity as the soul of holiness Love of God and neighbor is the heart of sanctification; martyrdom as the highest witness; evangelical counsels (virginity, poverty, obedience) as signs of love

V: Religious

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 43 Evangelical counsels and their institutionalization The evangelical counsels (chastity, poverty, obedience) are a gift of God. The Church establishes various forms of religious life supporting perfection and fraternal community.
LG 44 The nature of religious profession The vows bind one to total self-offering to God. Religious life is a visible sign for the Church: it shows heavenly goods, gives witness to new life, and demonstrates Christ’s power.
LG 45 The role of the Church’s hierarchy The hierarchy wisely directs the practice of evangelical counsels and approves rules. The Pope may exempt Institutes from diocesan jurisdiction; religious show reverence and obedience to bishops.
LG 46 The task of religious Religious show Christ: contemplating, preaching, healing, converting. The evangelical counsels purify the heart and give spiritual freedom for deeper love of God.
LG 47 The call to perseverance Each called person should persevere and advance in their vocation for the holiness of the Church and the glory of the Holy Trinity.

VII: The Eschatological Character of the Pilgrim Church and Its Union with the Heavenly Church

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 48 The eschatological character of the Church; renewal of the world in Christ The Church will reach its fullness in heavenly glory. Renewal has already begun in Christ and continues through the Holy Spirit. The pilgrim Church possesses imperfect holiness as yet; it awaits new heavens and a new earth.
LG 49 The communion of the pilgrim Church with the Church in heaven All united in Christ—pilgrims on earth, the deceased being purified, the blessed in heaven—form one Church. Saints intercede for us; their care assists our weakness.
LG 50 The veneration of saints and their role in the Church’s life We honor the memory of the deceased and pray for them. Saints show us the path to perfect union with Christ. The veneration of saints leads us to Christ and enriches Eucharistic worship.
LG 51 The Church’s teaching on the communion of saints; purity of its practice The Council accepts the faith in living fellowship with the inhabitants of heaven. True veneration of saints consists in increasing active charity and imitating their example, not in a multitude of external acts.

VIII: The Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of God in the Mystery of Christ and the Church

Paragraph Number Main Topic Key Thought
LG 52 Introduction: Mary in the mystery of salvation and the Church God willed to save the world through the Incarnation. Mary as Mother of God and Mother of the Redeemer holds the first place in the Church’s veneration after Christ.
LG 53 The dignity and role of Mary in the economy of salvation Mary—Mother of God and of the Redeemer, redeemed in a more exalted way, endowed with the highest dignity. United with all people through descent from Adam; mother of the members of Christ and model of faith and charity.
LG 54 The scope of the Council’s teaching on Mary The Council explains Mary’s role in the mystery of the Incarnate Word and the Church, but does not intend to present a complete Mariology. Theologians remain free in their reflections on the One who holds the highest place after Christ.
LG 55 Mary in the Old and New Testament; preparation of the economy of salvation Old Testament prophecies gradually reveal Mary as the Mother of the Savior. She holds the first place among the humble and poor of the Lord; with her comes the fullness of time and the new economy of salvation.
LG 56 The Immaculate One; the will of the Father and Mary’s cooperation in salvation The Father willed that the Incarnation be preceded by Mary’s consent. Endowed with special holiness, conceived without the stain of sin, she responds to the Annunciation with complete obedience. Not passively, but actively she cooperates in the work of salvation (parallel: Eve–Mary).
LG 57 The bond of Mary with her Son from conception to death From virginal conception through birth, to the temple, to the public teaching—Mary accompanied her Son. At the cross she stood without God’s decree having imposed it, she suffered with him, and was given as mother to the disciple.
LG 58 Mary in the public life of Jesus; her attitude of faith At Cana in Galilee she interceded for those in need. She watched over her Son’s teaching, which places heavenly ties above bonds of blood. She journeyed onward in the pilgrimage of faith until the cross, where she co-offered her Son.
LG 59 Mary after the Resurrection; the Immaculate Conception; the Assumption On the day of Pentecost she remained with the Apostles in prayer. Immaculate from the first moment of conception, having completed her life on earth, she was taken up into heavenly glory, exalted as Queen.
LG 60 The unique mediation of Christ and the role of Mary Mary does not dim the mediation of Christ but reveals his power. Her saving influence flows from God’s good pleasure and draws from the merits of Christ; it strengthens (does not hinder) the direct union of the faithful with Christ.
LG 61 Mary—Mother in the order of grace Mary, destined to be the Mother of God, became in a special way the cooperator of the Savior through faith, hope, and charity. In conceiving, nourishing, offering Christ, and suffering with him at the cross—she became mother to us in the order of grace.
LG 62 The motherhood of Mary in the economy of grace—unceasing From the Annunciation to eternity: Mary persists in her saving task. In heaven through her manifold intercession she obtains for us the gifts of salvation. She cares for the brothers and sisters of her Son, pilgrims on earth. Titles: Advocate, Helper, Auxiliary, Mediatrix (these diminish nothing from Christ).
LG 63 Mary—the type (pattern) of the Church Mary, united with her Son the Redeemer through motherhood, is deeply bound to the Church. She is the first exemplar of faith, charity, and perfect union with Christ. She stands first as a model of virginity and also of motherhood.
LG 64 The Church imitates Mary and becomes mother and virgin The Church, contemplating the mystery of Mary, imitating her charity and fulfilling the will of the Father, itself becomes mother (through preaching and baptism) and virgin (preserving inviolate faith, hope, and charity).
LG 65 Mary—model of virtues for the pilgrim Church While Mary in the Church already achieves perfection, Christians continually strive to grow in holiness, lifting their eyes to Mary as a model. Contemplating her, the Church penetrates more deeply into the mystery of the Incarnation and conforms itself to Christ.
LG 66 The cult of Mary—special, not identical with the cult of God Mary, exalted above all after her Son, rightly receives special veneration. Since the Council of Ephesus, a cult expressing itself in honor, love, invocations, and imitation. A unique cult, essentially different from the cult of adoration (offered to Christ).
LG 67 Instruction on the cult of Mary and its proper practice The Council earnestly recommends supporting the cult of Mary, especially the liturgical, and valuing pious practices. It encourages theologians and preachers: to explain Mary’s gifts without exaggeration or narrowness, always relating them to Christ. True piety flows from faith.
LG 68 Mary—sign of certain hope for the pilgrims Mary, already enjoying glory in heaven (in body and soul), is the image of the future perfection of the Church. On earth she shines upon the pilgrim People of God as a sign of certain hope and consolation.
LG 69 Call to prayer and unity All Christians should beseech Mary, Mother of God and of mankind, that she intercede for us within the Communion of Saints. The prayers of Mary sustained the beginning of the Church; may they support all humanity toward peace and unity in one People of God.
